## 6. What must be true

1. The lot price, the owner's share or the infrastructure cost has to move: at the middle of the reported range the lots of the neighbourhood barely cover the money that built it.
2. A single buy-back date at 12 months doesn't fit lots sold in instalments: the trust owes the money before the buyers have paid it.
3. Paying investors as the lot money arrives removes the date risk; what is left is the sales risk.
4. The commons can't live on lots: the toll comes once and dues are small. It needs its own income (stays, kitchen, workshops, dues from more neighbourhoods).
