# 0. Executive synthesis

Lighthouse should be modeled as a **regenerative territorial metabolism**, not primarily as a real-estate project. The proposed unit of analysis is:

**hectares -> ecosystems -> communities -> production -> human needs -> cash/internal/ecological flows**.

The strategic proposition is a **federation of intentional communities with distinct productive/cultural purposes**, sharing common metabolic infrastructure: water, energy, food logistics, sanitation, mobility, health, education, emergency capacity, digital systems, governance and finance.

Auroville is useful as a reference for distributed settlements plus shared services, but also as a warning: land availability alone does not create food sovereignty. Auroville's official sources describe a 2,000 ha planned township, a 50,000-person target, multiple functional zones, a green belt and ~120 settlements; its own farming sources state that local farms cover only a fraction of total food demand, depending on season and category. This supports designing **production, processing, distribution, demand and governance as one system**, not merely allocating hectares to farming.

The current NetX financial model is a useful platform/economic prototype, but it should not yet be treated as a Lighthouse-specific underwriting model. Its base land allocation is 51% Natural Assets, 25% Housing/Workspaces, 15% Energy, 5% Events, 3% Eco-production and 1% Wellness. For a 1,000-person intentional-community federation, the model currently under-allocates land to provisioning and over-specifies land for energy without a site-derived resource plan. It also projects territorial growth far beyond a fixed 1,000 ha site, so a bounded Lighthouse case is needed.

Recommended planning anchor:

- 1,000 ha.
- ~1,000 permanent residents as an initial full-build reference, not a target to force.
- 10-12 intentional communities, generally 70-110 residents each.
- 3-5 walkable community clusters rather than continuous suburbia.
- >50% ecological/productive landscape with strong continuity.
- ~180-250 ha of actively food-oriented production as a serious food-resilience envelope.
- shared infrastructure rather than duplicating services in every community.
- success measured partly by **needs coverage and commons resilience**, not only land appreciation and transaction revenue.

# 1. Planning concept

## 1.1 Proposed name for the model

**Regenerative Territorial Metabolism Model (RTMM)**

Alternative internal names:
- Full-Spectrum Territorial Model
- Regenerative Community-of-Communities Model
- Lighthouse Living Systems Model

RTMM is preferred because it makes explicit that the territory has inputs, stocks, flows, outputs and regenerative feedback loops.

## 1.2 Core design hypothesis

Lighthouse is not one intentional community. It is a **community of communities**.

Each community:
- has a distinct purpose and stewardship domain;
- provides something useful to the federation;
- retains meaningful cultural and governance autonomy;
- participates in shared agreements for land, water, ecological corridors, infrastructure and minimum service obligations.

The federation shares:
- food aggregation/distribution;
- water systems;
- renewable energy and microgrid functions;
- sanitation and circular-resource systems;
- mobility and logistics;
- health and emergency capacity;
- schooling/learning infrastructure;
- governance, conflict transformation and legal interfaces;
- accounting/marketplace/treasury;
- selected heavy equipment, storage and processing.

# 2. Reference: Auroville - what to borrow and what not to assume

## 2.1 Useful structural lessons

Auroville's official masterplan describes:
- a planned 2,000 ha township;
- an ultimate population of 50,000;
- four major functional zones around a central peace area;
- roughly 25% built-up area surrounded by a green belt;
- integrated work on afforestation, water, energy, land regeneration and community participation.

Auroville's planning pages also describe approximately 120 settlements of varying size. This reinforces a key Lighthouse pattern: **distributed communities inside a larger civic/ecological system**.

## 2.2 Food-system warning

Auroville's own farming material states that only part of food demand is met internally. One official farming page reports ~20% in the best harvest season and ~5% in summer; another official page states that over 320 acres of farmland exist but only about two thirds are under active food cultivation.

Implication:

**food sovereignty requires more than farmland.** It requires:
1. agronomic suitability;
2. reliable water;
3. growers and labor;
4. storage and processing;
5. logistics;
6. demand alignment/diet;
7. viable farm economics;
8. governance of commons and risk.

Lighthouse should therefore model the complete food metabolism, including what is intentionally sourced from the wider 10,000 ha BioPueblo territory and regional market.

# 3. Human-needs frame: Barrett's Seven Levels

Richard Barrett's Seven Levels of Consciousness model extends/reframes Maslow into:

1. Survival
2. Relationship
3. Self-esteem
4. Transformation
5. Internal cohesion
6. Making a difference
7. Service

The first three center on deficiency needs; Transformation bridges toward the higher growth levels; the upper three center on meaning, contribution and service.

For Lighthouse, use Barrett as a **system-coverage test**, not as seven physically separate communities.

| Level | Territorial question | Examples of Lighthouse capabilities | Candidate KPI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Survival | Can people reliably live here? | water, food, shelter, energy, sanitation, basic health, financial security | % potable water secured; % food categories covered; energy autonomy; emergency reserves |
| 2 Relationship | Can people belong and feel safe? | communities, common kitchens, plazas, mutual aid, childcare | retention; participation; care coverage; conflict safety indicators |
| 3 Self-esteem | Can people contribute and be recognized? | meaningful work, enterprises, roles, apprenticeships | % residents with viable livelihood; local procurement; skills pathways |
| 4 Transformation | Can people grow in autonomy? | education, mobility between roles, governance learning, mediation | learning hours; role mobility; governance participation |
| 5 Internal cohesion | Can people live with meaning? | arts, spiritual/contemplative spaces, nature connection, purpose work | resident-defined purpose/wellbeing indicators |
| 6 Making a difference | Does Lighthouse contribute outside itself? | research, training, regenerative enterprises, external services | external learners/customers; replicated practices; regional partnerships |
| 7 Service | Does the system leave the bioregion better? | rewilding, open knowledge, biodiversity, long-horizon stewardship | soil/water/biodiversity trends; protected corridors; intergenerational assets |

Important: **resilience is not autarky**. Lighthouse does not need to locally manufacture every medicine, machine, metal, electronic component or food category. The objective is critical-system autonomy plus deliberate regional interdependence.

## 4.2 Proposed Lighthouse allocation using permaculture logic

This is a planning envelope, not a site plan. Final distribution must follow water, soils, topography, flood risk, wind, frost, ecology and access.

| Zone / function | ha | % | Role |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Z0 - settlements + civic commons | 70 | 7% | 10-12 communities, shared civic nodes, housing/workspaces |
| Z1 - intensive production | 50 | 5% | gardens, greenhouses, nurseries, medicinal crops |
| Z2 - agroforestry/orchards | 110 | 11% | fruit, nuts, berries, poultry, integrated perennial systems |
| Z3 - field crops/silvopasture | 190 | 19% | staples, legumes, forage, rotational livestock |
| Z4 - managed productive landscape | 180 | 18% | timber, fibers, biomaterials, grazing, biochar, low-intensity harvest |
| Z5 - conservation/rewilding | 330 | 33% | habitat, biodiversity, riparian/ecological corridors, restoration |
| Shared infrastructure | 70 | 7% | water, sanitation, roads, logistics, energy, service infrastructure |
| **Total** | **1,000** | **100%** | |

Key property: **Z4 + Z5 = 510 ha**, preserving the same 51% landscape share currently carried as Natural Assets in the financial model, while radically reallocating the remaining 49% toward a living-system logic.

Note: energy, wellness, events and much infrastructure should often be **embedded** into settlement/productive landscapes rather than treated as large isolated mono-use land banks.

- Regenerative production yield: **6,000 kg/ha/year**.
- Total physical output: **180,000 kg/year**.

Do **not** convert these kg figures directly into people fed. Nutritional value differs radically between vegetables, fruit, grains, oil, dairy and protein. A proper model must use calories/macros/micronutrients and seasonal crop plans.

A reasonable next planning envelope is **180-250 ha actively food-oriented**, while additional Z4 land may produce timber, forage, fiber, biomass or ecological services.

## 7.4 50-ha high-value production example

Mechanical extrapolation of the workbook base assumptions:

- 50 ha x 6,000 kg = **300 t/year**.
- gross modeled value: **~$2.955M/year**.
- operating costs: **~$798k/year**.
- modeled CapEx package: **~$787.5k**.
- NetX eco-production take rate in base model: **5% = ~$147.8k** on gross.
- 21% IVA line in current model would equal **~$620.6k** if applied to the gross base exactly as modeled.

This illustrates why not all productive land should be modeled identically. A better portfolio could distinguish:
- high-value horticulture / value-added production;
- food-security staples;
- orchard/perennials;
- forage/protein;
- ecological biomaterials;
- experimental/rotation land.

# 8. Energy - challenge to current 15% allocation

The model allocates 15% of total land to energy and, in base assumptions, distributes this as 5% solar, 5% wind and 5% hydro.

Base solar assumptions:
- 300 kW installed capacity per solar hectare;
- 20% capacity factor.

Illustrative arithmetic:
- 10 equivalent solar ha -> 3 MW installed.
- 3 MW x 20% x 8,760 h -> **~5.26 GWh/year**.
- at 1,000 residents -> **~5,256 kWh/person/year** before industrial/community loads and losses.

Implication: a full 150-ha exclusive energy bank may be unnecessary for community resilience unless Lighthouse deliberately intends to be a major energy exporter or host energy-intensive activity.

Recommended modeling change:
- distinguish **exclusive energy footprint** from embedded generation (rooftops, parking, agrivoltaics, service buildings);
- derive solar/wind/hydro shares from site resource studies rather than fixed equal percentages;
- model community load, storage, peak demand, backup and grid/export conditions before assigning hectares.

# 9. The 10,000-ha BioPueblo context

Lighthouse should not optimize for total self-sufficiency if it is embedded in a larger ~10,000 ha mosaic of BioPueblo projects.

Use a layered resilience strategy:

## 9.1 Critical capabilities to secure locally or contractually
- potable water and emergency water;
- essential food categories;
- electricity/microgrid resilience;
- sanitation and waste systems;
- primary repair capability;
- emergency response;
- basic care and schooling/learning capacity;
- ecological corridor protection.

## 9.2 Good candidates for interdependence with the wider 10,000 ha
- grains or forage at scale;
- extensive livestock;
- timber and structural biomaterials;
- heavy machinery;
- large cold storage/processing;
- renewable generation/export;
- transport/logistics;
- specialized education or health services.

## 9.3 Likely regional/global dependencies
- specialized medicine;
- electronics;
- metals and machinery;
- some fuels/backup systems;
- specialized seeds/inputs;
- advanced tertiary education/hospital care.

Strategic positioning option:

**Lighthouse = the community-of-communities / full-spectrum living node inside the BioPueblo 10,000-ha ecosystem.**

Its differentiator is not merely regenerative land sales, but demonstrating how people can actually live, work, produce, govern and care for one another after the land transaction.

# 10. Challenges to the current NetX Financial Model

These are design questions, not criticisms of the model's usefulness. The existing workbook remains valuable as an exploratory NetX revenue model.

## Challenge 1 - Separate NetX network economics from Lighthouse project economics

The workbook's Charts sheet reaches **~16,022 hectares developed by 2030**, far beyond a fixed 1,000-ha Lighthouse site. Therefore, the model currently mixes or prioritizes a scalable network/platform case.

**Change requested:** create a bounded 1,000-ha Lighthouse model alongside the NetX platform/network model.

## Challenge 2 - Land allocation is inherited, not mission-derived

The current 5/1/25/3/15/51 allocation is explicitly noted in the workbook as based on Molvento specifications.

**Change requested:** derive Lighthouse land use from hydrology, ecology, food system, community design and population scenarios; then feed that allocation into economics.

## Challenge 3 - 3% eco-production is incompatible with meaningful provisioning

At 1,000 ha, it provides only 30 productive ha. Under the workbook's own assumptions, 20% internal allocation gives only 36 t/year for community use.

**Change requested:** separate food-security hectares from commercial eco-production hectares. Internal provisioning should be modeled as both avoided expenditure and resilience, not only as a sales-like revenue stream.

## Challenge 4 - Internal consumption appears inside Gross Revenues

The Eco-production projection counts internal consumption as a gross-revenue line and applies sales tax/IVA at the aggregate level.

This may be appropriate if internal consumption is actually invoiced through a market or service entity, but it should not be assumed automatically.

**Change requested:** distinguish:
- external cash sales;
- internal transfer prices;
- household/community provisioning;
- avoided-cost value;
- taxable vs non-taxable flows.

Tax treatment must be validated locally.

## Challenge 5 - Land appreciation dominates the modeled revenue mix

In the Charts sheet's 2030 view, land appreciation contributes roughly **40.1% of modeled Net Revenues** ($3.752M of $9.351M shown).

This is a legitimate development-stage value engine, but it creates a strategic question:

**What finances the commons after land-sale/appreciation capture matures?**

**Change requested:** add a post-development steady-state case where land-sale dependence is low or zero.

## Challenge 6 - Energy land allocation is not site-derived

The model allocates 5% each to solar, wind and hydro, regardless of actual site suitability, rights, grid access or hydrology.

**Change requested:** resource-based energy model + load model + embedded generation before land allocation.

## Challenge 7 - Same land allocation across Upside/Base/Conservative

Land-use percentages remain the same across scenarios. This means scenario analysis primarily changes prices/yields/costs rather than the territorial strategy itself.

**Change requested:** add structural scenarios, e.g.:
- Development-led;
- Community-resilience-led;
- Production/export-led;
- Conservation/research-led.

## Challenge 8 - Productive-land economics need crop/enterprise portfolios

A single 6,000 kg/ha yield and a single channel basket is useful for early modeling but cannot represent orchards, vegetables, grains, livestock, fibers and timber simultaneously.

**Change requested:** replace one eco-production archetype with 5-7 enterprise archetypes, each with:
- hectares;
- maturity curve;
- seasonal yield;
- labor;
- CapEx;
- water demand;
- processing/storage;
- internal consumption;
- external revenue;
- risk.

## Challenge 9 - The model needs a water balance

For an irrigated/regenerative territory in Río Negro, water can become the principal carrying-capacity constraint.

**Change requested:** add annual/monthly water budget by:
- domestic;
- agricultural;
- livestock;
- processing;
- wellness/hospitality;
- ecological restoration;
- fire reserve;
- losses/reuse.

## Challenge 10 - Housing density should follow community form, not only units/ha

The model contains a units-per-hectare assumption for governance/membership economics, but Lighthouse needs household mix, community clusters and occupancy dynamics.

**Change requested:** model households/residents first, then built area, infrastructure and memberships.

## Challenge 11 - Commons must appear as an economic subsystem

Shared kitchens, care, food logistics, education, water, mobility and governance create real value but may look like cost centers in a conventional P&L.

**Change requested:** explicitly model commons funding sources and service obligations.

## Challenge 12 - Add ecological carrying-capacity constraints and asset trajectories

Natural Assets are currently monetized mainly through traceability/token logic.

**Change requested:** also track the underlying ecological state: soil organic matter, groundwater/water quality, biodiversity, canopy, erosion, habitat connectivity and restoration cost/liability.

# 11. Recommended next-generation financial architecture

Use **three ledgers**, then consolidate.

## Ledger A - Cash economy
Tracks actual money:
- external sales;
- hospitality/events;
- land/lease revenue;
- memberships;
- energy export;
- products/services;
- grants/investment;
- payroll, purchases, taxes and debt.

## Ledger B - Internal provisioning / commons economy
Tracks value that may not be external revenue:
- food supplied to residents;
- childcare/eldercare;
- shared transport;
- housing subsidy or common facilities;
- repair and maintenance exchange;
- education;
- volunteer/stewardship commitments;
- avoided external expenditure.

Purpose: distinguish **value created** from **cash collected**.

## Ledger C - Ecological capital
Tracks biophysical performance:
- soil health;
- water balance;
- biodiversity;
- carbon/biomass where meaningful;
- habitat connectivity;
- productive capacity;
- restoration liabilities.

Purpose: prevent profitability from being achieved by quietly degrading the asset that sustains the community.

4. soil and productive capability;

7. access and infrastructure;
8. high-value/intensive productive zones;
9. settlement clusters;
10. energy systems;
11. public/commons facilities;

# 17. Working thesis

**Lighthouse can be positioned as a 1,000-ha prototype for full-spectrum regenerative living: approximately 1,000 residents in 10-12 intentional communities, over half the territory held in ecological/productive landscape, ~180-250 ha of serious food-oriented production, shared metabolic infrastructure, and an economy that measures success by the degree to which recurring activity can sustain everyday life and commons after land-sale revenues decline.**

Within the wider ~10,000-ha BioPueblo ecosystem, Lighthouse's most differentiated role could be:

**the community-of-communities node that demonstrates the social, economic and ecological operating system of regenerative settlement.**

# 19. Explicit limitations
